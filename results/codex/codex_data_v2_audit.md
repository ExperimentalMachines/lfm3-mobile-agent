# v2 SFT data audit — 2026-11-17

**Verdict: NO-GO for training the published v2 revision.** There is confirmed train/dev screen leakage, demonstrably incompatible action supervision, and premature completion labels. Valid JSON and matching builder statistics do not clear this mix.

Audited dataset: `experimentalmachines/AndroidAgent-SFT-v2` at **`8e0e63a48f180aa60c103d2f492d6364355cb306`**. Downloaded exactly **three v2 train shards, 00000/00003/00004**, selected with seed `20261117`, and all three dev shards: **15,000 train + 1,999 dev = 16,999 rows**. Dataset files/card/stats totaled **1,626,522,302 bytes**. The remaining 13 train shards were not downloaded or inspected. Findings are confirmed instances or explicitly bounded sample results, not a certification of all 79,991 advertised train rows.

I decoded and hashed every downloaded image, ran the actual strict action parser and resize rule, joined primary-source annotations, inspected **40 random train rows per new source**, and performed 27 additional targeted review instances, some overlapping the random sample, plus five AC screens for app-identity review. Red crosshairs use `agent_format.norm_to_px`; images were not generated or altered except for analytical overlays. No GPU training or live device replay was performed.

**Quotas and caps.** The quota-as-ceiling rule works for the current disjoint predicates: filler skips every quota category. Reconstructing AMEX's entire small annotation archive yields **34,450 train candidates** and, before image validation, 8,000 selected rows: 500 Enter, 500 complete, 150 infeasible, 1,000 type, 4,424 click, 1,406 scroll, 18 back, 2 home. This matches the builder's claimed 7,998 materialized rows after its reported removal of one click and one infeasible row; those two image removals were not independently checked against raw media. Every inspected train AMEX key belongs to the reconstructed selection. The cap defect is in the quota passes, not the filler; fixing it can preserve the allocation without adding duplicate rows. ScreenQA's observed train per-screen cap and Odyssey's observed train per-episode cap hold in this sample. AC has no episode cap and uses uniform row sampling rather than the plan's app/goal/action balancing.

**Implications for the 2B model.** The promising additions are real screen answers, diverse apps, and explicit actions absent from v1. The most concrete regression risks are premature stopping, silent responses to information requests, typing special-key names, learning scrolls the executor cannot perform, and double submission after typing. Generic AMEX thoughts also weaken the useful target/state descriptions that v1 puts into history; blanking them avoids supervising meaningless prose but does not magically supply missing state. Odyssey histories average **5.30 entries / 688 user-text characters**, versus AC **3.29 / 436**; verify cross-app memory sufficiency and actual token throughput rather than assuming a row always costs the same GPU time. No accuracy improvement or regression was measured here. After repair, use the plan's equal-budget AC-only control on clean development splits, and keep frozen AC TEST/AW outcomes out of mix tuning.

**Final decision: NO-GO.** Rebuild with a screen-disjoint dev split; resolve the type/Enter executor contract; quarantine unsupported gestures/key-like text and invalid trajectory suffixes; review terminal and answer semantics; resolve held-out app provenance; enforce caps in quota selection.
