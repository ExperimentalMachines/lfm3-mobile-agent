**New issues and effects of the fixes.**

1. **New OCR failure handling defect — medium, reproducible in code, occurrence in this build unproven.** At `data/build_v2_mix.py:164`, `subprocess.run` does not use `check=True` or inspect `returncode`. A simulated Tesseract exit **7** with empty stdout makes `_ocr_pending` return **False**, so the terminal passes the pending-screen check; it does not enter `terminal_ocr_failed`. Timeouts/missing executable exceptions do get quarantined. Check process success and meaningful OCR output before treating a result as reviewed. There is no evidence here that the actual builder's Tesseract failed; zero logged exceptions would not detect this failure path. [Executed fixture](data_v2_cache/verify_d33cb3c217/new_filter_probes.json).

2. **New near-miss filter removes valid work — medium quality/coverage issue.** It compares typed text with same-token-count goal spans using an 0.8 similarity threshold, which does not distinguish typos from paraphrases, inflections, or useful query expansions. Real source episode `0080420605470053` truncates at “Chocolate chip cookies” for a goal saying “chocolate chip cookie recipe”; `0785417706556484` rejects “properties of cube” for a goal saying “properties of a Cube.” Both are sensible searches. Among the 2,957 inspected Odyssey source annotation files, I recorded **277 text cutoffs** and **441 additional band-rule cutoffs**; these are observations from selected episodes, not whole-source error-rate estimates. The full-build **714 Odyssey / 134 AMEX** key/empty/typo rejection counters cannot be interpreted as that many genuine mistakes. Conservatively dropping uncertain data is a policy choice; calling this heuristic reliable typo detection is incorrect. Restrict exact-copy checks to identifiable entities/quoted strings or review rejected query classes. [Real rejected examples](data_v2_cache/verify_d33cb3c217/odyssey_provenance.json), [fixtures](data_v2_cache/verify_d33cb3c217/new_filter_probes.json).

3. **Quota shortfall and changed action coverage — documented outcome, not a 40,000-row budget blocker.** AMEX selection reaches **7,472/8,000 before image checks**, then **7,451** after 1 landscape, 17 pending-terminal and 3 frame drops, then **7,450** after the dev-image exclusion. The cap/category-ceiling combination exhausts eligible filler; it is not simply an OCR loss. Only **13 Enter targets** remain, versus 500 previously. The card truthfully reports final counts, and those quotas are ceilings, not requirements to fabricate examples. Do not describe this as 500 standalone Enter examples or an exact 80K mixture. Total train shortfall is **680/80,000 (0.85%)**. Terminals remain 610 Odyssey + 632 AMEX, so the increased pre-OCR ceiling of 650 did not just replace the removed infeasible rows with a shortage. The stale builder header still describes older terminal quotas and infeasible mappings; update that documentation.

   The scroll filter **did not remove most published scroll supervision**: AMEX remains **1,406** train scrolls; Odyssey falls **910→672**. Combined new-source scrolls are **2,078 versus 2,316**, a 10.3% decrease. Candidate pools do shrink substantially: AMEX **34,450→26,010**, Odyssey **65,379→47,242**, from the combined filters. These figures do not isolate the band rule's causal effect. Counts of truncated episodes alone are not counts of lost scroll targets.
