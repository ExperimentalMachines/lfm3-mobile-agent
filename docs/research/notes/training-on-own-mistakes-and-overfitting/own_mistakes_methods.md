# Training a small GUI agent on its own mistakes without an online environment or a large teacher (as of 2026-11-21)

Scope and conventions:
- Every number below is **reported by the paper's authors** unless marked otherwise. I found **no independent reproduction** of any of these training results. The only third-party numbers are re-evaluations of released checkpoints, which are flagged where used.
- **Model sizes** are given for every effect. Effects measured on models **larger than 8B** are marked **(>8B)**.
- Material already covered in `docs/research/small-gui-agent-training-methods.md` and its notes (UI-S1 basics, GUI-Reflection, OpenMobile, Xiaomi-GUI-0 masking, DigiRL, offline-GRPO pitfalls) is only summarised here, with new details added.
- "Teacher-free" means no external model is used to label, judge or correct. A model that shares weights with the student (a "self-teacher") counts as teacher-free.
- Project numbers (21% `wait` under a non-wait intent; 73% of consecutive steps repeating the previous intent word for word; static GRPO lowering AndroidControl step accuracy from 0.728 to 0.667) come from the task brief, not from a published source.

## Q1. Semi-online RL (UI-S1): how it simulates online rollouts on static trajectories, what it needs, what it gains, whether code exists

### Takeaway
UI-S1 gives the model its **own** previous thoughts and actions as history, but only shows it the **gold** next screenshot. When the model's action does not match the gold action, a patch module puts in the gold action and carries on. Rewards are discounted over the stretch of steps where the model kept matching.
- At 7B it took AndroidWorld (AW) from 21.7 (SFT) to 34.0, using AndroidControl (AC) train plus AMEX, the same sources we train on.
- At 3B, without an SFT start, AW went from 5.0 to 13.1.
- Code (verl-based), the 7B checkpoint and 1,000 training examples are public.
- The paper trained on 32 GPUs. It shows a GPU-hours figure, but no number for it could be extracted. No result below 3B exists, and nobody has independently reproduced the training.

### Cited Findings
**Paper and release status**
- UI-S1, arXiv 2509.11543. v1 2025-09-15, v2 2025-09-24. Accepted to ACL 2026 main (README news item dated 2026-04-06). — [UI-S1 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543); [UI-S1 README](https://github.com/X-PLUG/MobileAgent/tree/main/UI-S1)
- Release timeline from the README (accessed 2026-11-21, last commit 2026-11-03):
  - Training and evaluation code released 2025-09-17. It is built on verl and verl-agent, with a vLLM 0.8.2 server.
  - UI-S1-7B checkpoint released 2025-09-16.
  - "Part of our training dataset" (1,000 examples) released 2025-10-28.
  - Training runs via `bash scripts/train_example.sh`; the repo also has a 32B example script.
  - Source: [UI-S1 README](https://github.com/X-PLUG/MobileAgent/tree/main/UI-S1)

**Rollout mechanics (Algorithm 1)**
- Start from an expert trajectory τ* = {(S₁*, a₁*), …}. Sample N rollouts from the current policy. Each rollout keeps its own history H_t^i = {(S, a^i, T^i)}, where T is the model's thought.
- If Matches(a_t^i, a_t*), the next state is the expert's S_{t+1}*. Otherwise the next state is None, and the rollout either ends or is patched.
- The paper's motivation: static training conditions on H_static = {(S*, a*)}, but deployment conditions on the model's own outputs. Statically trained agents therefore "never learn to process their own outputs or recover from errors."
- Source: [UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)
- **Patch module.** On a mismatch, the gold action replaces the wrong one, together with a "thought" that is empty (thought-free), generated by another model (off-policy), or generated by the policy itself when shown the gold action (on-policy). This is allowed up to ε times per rollout. The final recipe is thought-free with ε=1. — [UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)

  | Patch variant (1K data, ε=∞) | SOP score |
  |---|---|
  | Thought-free | 25.7 |
  | Off-policy (DeepSeek-R1 thoughts) | 22.6 |
  | On-policy | 26.1 |

  Source: [UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543) (prior project notes)

**Rewards and advantages**
- Step reward: 0.1·format + 0.4·type + 0.5·exact match, where each later term counts only if the earlier ones pass.
- Return: R_t = Σ γ^(k−t) r_k, summed only to t_end, the last step of the **naturally matching** segment.
- Advantage: A = A_episode + ω·A_step, where A_step is normalised across rollouts at the same step. Groups are resampled until the advantage standard deviation exceeds η = 0.3, a DAPO-style filter.
- Source: [UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)
- Ending the return at the natural segment beats ending it at the full patched length T. AW: 28.0 vs 27.9 (ε=1), 28.9 vs 27.7 (ε=2), 28.4 vs 27.4 (ε=∞). — [UI-S1 Table 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)
- γ=0.5 beats γ=0: SOP-PG is about 26-28 vs about 21-23 across the ω/η grid (1K data, 1 epoch, ε=0). — [UI-S1 Table 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)

**Training hyperparameters (Table 9, UI-S1-7B)**
- Pipeline: SFT of Qwen2.5-VL-7B on AC-Train + AMEX, then semi-online RL.
- Settings: train batch 32, lr 1e-6, KL loss coefficient 1e-4, γ 0.5, ω 1.0, ε 1, η 0.3, **2 history images**, max prompt 12,288 tokens, max response 512, 5 epochs.
- Hardware: **4 nodes × 8 GPUs**.
- A "training GPU hours" figure (Fig. 11) exists, but its values are not in the text.
- Source: [UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)

**Data efficiency**
- At 7B with the thought-free patch at ε=1, AW was 24.0 / 29.1 / 34.0 with 200 / 500 / 1,000 trajectories. — [UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543) (prior project notes)

**Successor**
- SOLAR-RL (7B) also rebuilds 8 candidate rollouts per step from static data, finds the first failure point, and redistributes dense step rewards. It reports AW 33.7. — [SOLAR-RL, arXiv 2604.22558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22558) (prior project notes)

### Inferences
- The rollout keeps every screenshot from the gold trajectory, so the gold label stays valid for the screen shown. Only the *text history* is the model's own. For our failure mode (copying the previous intent, 73%), that is the right place to intervene: the model sees its own copied intents in history and is still supervised to output the gold next step.
- An SFT version needs no RL stack. Roll the current model through AC, GUI-Odyssey and AMEX *train* episodes with its own intent history. At the first mismatch, save (own history, gold screen, gold label) as an SFT row, patch with the gold action, and continue. This uses UI-S1's state distribution with a plain SFT loss. UI-S1 never tested an SFT loss on these states, so the gain is unknown.
- A full UI-S1 port to LFM3-VL-2B means adding the model to verl and vLLM. The paper used 32 GPUs for 5 epochs at 7B. A 1-day, 1×A100 budget at 2B is plausible only with far less data (UI-S1 got AW 24.0 from 200 trajectories at 7B) and likely fewer epochs. This is an extrapolation, not a reported number.
- UI-S1's SFT stage *lowered* MiniWob++, and offline GRPO barely moved AW. Both match our own observation that offline GRPO can lower AC step accuracy (0.728 to 0.667).

### Gaps
- No GPU-hour number, no per-trajectory rollout count N, and no wall-clock time could be extracted (Fig. 11 is an image).
- No UI-S1 result exists below 3B, and none for SFT followed by semi-online training at 3B.
- No independent reproduction of UI-S1 *training* was found. The only third-party data point is VeriGUI's re-evaluation of the released checkpoint under a different protocol.
- The released dataset is "part of" the training set (1,000 examples). Whether it is enough to reproduce the AW 34.0 result is untested.

## Q2. DAgger, scheduled sampling and history perturbation for agents trained on static trajectories

### Takeaway
Within GUI agents, the only static-data methods that replace gold history with the model's own outputs are UI-S1 and its successor SOLAR-RL; both are RL methods. Two other GUI methods vary or corrupt the history without using the model's own outputs:
- VeriGUI puts a synthetic failed action into the history and keeps the screen unchanged.
- HiconAgent varies the history length during RL. This gave only +1.4 AC-High SR at 3B.

Classic DAgger variants for language agents (OEC, Revisiting DAgger, OpenMobile) all need an expert model *and* an environment.

Two results warn that self-history is fragile:
- In language agents, shuffling past actions barely changes success rates, so models make little use of what their past actions achieved. Explicitly labelling each observation as the outcome of the previous action reduces repeated actions.
- ReOPD finds that the more student-generated the history, the less reliable the teacher's label for it.

### Cited Findings
**GUI agents: self-history and history perturbation on static data**
- **UI-S1** is a form of student forcing: the model's own history plus gold screens, with a gold patch on mismatch. (See Q1.) — [UI-S1, 2509.11543, 2025-09-15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)
- **VeriGUI** (arXiv 2604.05477, 2026-04-07; Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 7B). This is synthetic negative history on AC with no rollouts.
  - "Type B" samples put the previous screen S_{t−1} (unchanged) in the input, with an erroneous action in the history. The target is a diagnosis followed by the correct action.
  - The authors also evaluate with a "pseudo-online simulation": a correct action advances to the gold next screen, a wrong action returns the same screen, and the episode is capped at 2× the gold length.
  - Source: [VeriGUI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05477)
- **HiconAgent** (arXiv 2512.01763, v1 2025-12-01, v2 2026-03-10; Qwen2.5-VL-3B, GRPO, 3K samples, 2 history steps with screenshots). Dynamic Context Sampling draws a different history length τ ∈ {0,1,2} for each rollout in a GRPO group. — [HiconAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01763)

  | Variant | AC-High SR | Train hours |
  |---|---|---|
  | No DCS | 51.03 | 17 |
  | Uniform τ, update on τ=2 only | 50.53 | 17 |
  | Uniform τ, update on all τ | 51.62 | 30 |
  | Exponentially biased toward long τ | 52.40 | 17 |

  - Uniform sampling caused "degeneration": the quality of short-history samples decayed during training. — [HiconAgent Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01763)
  - HiconAgent's analysis also found that history *actions* without their screenshots "lack grounded semantics and provide little guidance". — [HiconAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01763)

**DAgger-style methods for language agents (all need an expert and an environment)**
- **On-policy Expert Corrections (OEC)** (arXiv 2512.14895, 2025-12-16). The student starts the rollout and an expert takes over part-way. Trained by rejection-sampling SFT on SWE tasks: +14% relative at 7B and +13% at 32B **(>8B)** over plain imitation on SWE-bench Verified. — [OEC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14895)
- **Revisiting DAgger** (arXiv 2605.12913, 2026-05-13). Turn-level mixing of student and teacher policies, with teacher labels on the visited states. On SWE-bench Verified: +3.9 points at 4B (to 27.3%) and +3.6 at 8B (to 29.8%) over the strongest post-training baseline. — [Revisiting DAgger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12913)
- **OpenMobile** (arXiv 2604.15093, 7B). An expert takes over on deviation, and the learner's errors are kept in the history. AW 48.3, against 44.8 for pure expert distillation and 33.8 for self-evolution. Needs Gemini as the expert and an emulator. — [OpenMobile](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15093) (prior project notes)

**Offline replays of model-generated prefixes (background)**
- **ReOPD** (arXiv 2607.04763, 2026-07-06). Reuses pre-collected teacher trajectories as replayed prefixes. The student acts only at selected steps, and no tool calls are made during training. The authors name a "prefix trap": the more on-policy the history, the more relevant it is to the student, but the less reliable the teacher's target for it. Their fix is a step-decaying schedule that favours early prefixes. It runs at least 4× faster per rollout than online on-policy distillation. Needs a teacher. — [ReOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04763)
- **Batch-Scheduled Sampling** (arXiv 2410.14655, 2024-10-18). Offline, stochastically interleaves ground-truth tokens with the model's own generated tokens in the context. Reported as improving summarisation, QA and math over baselines. No agent results; no numbers extracted. — [Cen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14655)
- Outside agents, two methods train on perturbed self-generated histories:
  - Adversarial Gradient Forcing (video generation): trains on adversarially perturbed self-generated histories to close a "local history robustness gap". — [AGF, arXiv 2610.14553, 2026-10-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14553)
  - Gated Memory Policy (robotics): injects diffusion noise into historical actions "mitigating sensitivity to noisy or inaccurate histories". — [GMP, arXiv 2604.18933, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18933)

**Evidence that agents copy or ignore their own history**
- **When History Fails to Become Experience** (arXiv 2610.02769, 2026-10-02; language agents):
  - Shuffling past actions leaves task success "close to that with the original history".
  - Labelling each observation as "outcome of action A" (inference only, no training) cut the chance of repeating an action a third time after two identical actions:

  | Model | Raw | Outcome-labelled | Self-written experience |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Qwen3-8B | 51.4% | 45.3% | 25.2% |
  | Pooled, 5 models | 36.9% | 32.2% | 21.4% |

  - Keeping self-generated experiences for the *whole* episode lowered overall task success. A trained calibrator fixed this.
  - Source: [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02769)
- **What if LLMs Ate Their Words** (arXiv 2609.05882, 2026-09-05). Replacing the model's own earlier responses with neutral content raised downstream normalised performance by +0.027 across 2,973 conversations. In 63.7% of 237 degraded trajectories, at least one single-turn edit helped. — [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05882)
- **DeLoop** (arXiv 2610.19376; see Q3). Exact-repeat loops rise from 6.6% to 24.8% of actions when the history is compressed, so loops grow when the model cannot see clearly what it already did. — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19376)

### Inferences
- Our input has only a text history (8 intent/action pairs, no past screenshots). This is the setting where HiconAgent found history actions "lack grounded semantics", and where language agents ignore what their actions achieved. Copying the previous intent word for word is consistent with both findings.
- A cheap, teacher-free history-perturbation SFT for us would combine three things:
  1. With probability p, replace the gold history with the model's own predicted intents from a self-rollout (scheduled sampling at the step level).
  2. Insert VeriGUI-style failed actions with an unchanged screen.
  3. Drop or shorten the history at random, keeping the bias toward the full 8 steps, because HiconAgent found uniform sampling degraded results.

  No paper tests this exact combination on a ≤4B GUI agent, so its effect size is unknown.
- ReOPD's "prefix trap" matters less for UI-S1-style rollouts, because the screen is always gold. It matters a lot for any scheme that lets the student's *screens* drift, such as emulator rollouts relabelled by gold labels. There, the gold action may be wrong for the student's state.
- Outcome labelling in the history (e.g. "[no visible change]") is the inference-only analogue. The project's runtime-feedback run showed that adding these notes at test time without training does not help. The language-agent evidence suggests the labels need to be in training too.

### Gaps
- No GUI paper isolates "SFT on the model's own history from static trajectories" from the RL objective. UI-S1 has no SFT-loss ablation.
- No GUI paper reports scheduled-sampling (mixing rate p) ablations.
- No paper reports an intent-copying or repeated-thought metric for GUI agents before and after self-history training. VeriGUI's Loop Rate is the closest.

## Q3. Error-recovery, reflection, backtracking and hindsight data synthesised offline

### Takeaway
Three offline recipes are directly relevant to our `wait`/repeat failures.
- **VeriGUI (3B).** Adds 30% synthetic "failed previous action, unchanged screen" samples. One of its five failure types is exactly "`wait` when interaction is needed".
  - Effect: recovery success rate rose from 29.7% to 45.5% and the loop rate fell from 30.4% to 26.5%, at a 0.9-point cost in step SR.
  - With GRPO on top: SR 46.6, loop rate 24.3%, recovery success 51.1%.
  - Its failure-recovery samples were written by GPT-4o.
- **BacktrackAgent (7B).** Builds error-detection data from the agent's *own* regenerated wrong actions on the training set. It only helps when the real next page is known (+5.65 task success rate on Mobile3M). On chain datasets with simulated next pages it gave +0.7 and *lowered* accuracy.
- **DeLoop (8B text agents, no teacher, no judge).** Mines the agent's own loop-then-recover rollouts into DPO pairs. It cut loops from 57.3% to 6.1% of tasks without improving success, and it needs an environment.

Mobile-Agent-E is an inference-time memory framework for large models, not a training method.

### Cited Findings
**VeriGUI: robust SFT with synthetic failures, then GRPO with asymmetric rewards** (arXiv 2604.05477, 2026-04-07; Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B)
- **Data.** 70% normal steps (Type A). 30% "what if step t−1 failed" samples (Type B): previous screen unchanged, an erroneous action in the history, and the target is a structured diagnosis plus the correct action. — [VeriGUI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05477)
- **Failure types and sampling weights:**

  | Failure type | Weight | Description |
  |---|---|---|
  | Coordinate offset | 30% | Right element type, slightly misaligned tap |
  | Action-type error | 25% | Related but wrong type, e.g. long-press for click |
  | Wrong target | 20% | Visually similar but wrong element |
  | **Timing error** | **15%** | "uses a wait or similar temporal action when active interaction is required" |
  | Null click | 10% | Empty or non-interactive region |

  The weights are described as "empirically assigned". **GPT-4o generates** the erroneous action, both expected effects and the chain of thought. — [VeriGUI Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05477)
- **Stage ablation** (3B, AC-High; LR = loop rate, RSR = recovery success rate):

  | Stage | SR | Sim-TSR | LR ↓ | RSR |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Base | 31.2 | 0 | 30.0 | 35.0 |
  | Standard SFT | 42.6 | 10.0 | **30.4** | 29.7 |
  | Robust SFT (30% failures) | 41.7 | 13.3 | 26.5 | 45.5 |
  | + GRPO (VeriGUI-3B) | 46.6 | 16.7 | 24.3 | 51.1 |

  "Standard SFT … provides no verification benefit—Loop Rate actually increases slightly." — [VeriGUI Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05477)
- **Ratio of failure samples:**

  | Normal : failure | SR | RSR |
  |---|---|---|
  | 100:0 | 42.6 | 29.7 |
  | 90:10 | 42.1 | 30.3 |
  | 70:30 | 41.7 | 45.5 |
  | 50:50 | 39.2 | 45.4 |

  Too many negatives "degrade Step Accuracy". — [VeriGUI Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05477)
- **Reward ablation** (RSR): action reward only 44.9; + verification reward 48.1; + effect-prediction reward 51.1. — [VeriGUI Table 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05477)
- **Online results.** AW 12.6 and MiniWoB++ 35.6 at 3B; AW 25.1 and MiniWoB++ 59.7 at 7B. The authors say this beats all 3B baselines and UI-S1-7B, but the extract does not give same-harness 3B baseline AW numbers. — [VeriGUI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05477)
- **Cost.**
  - Hardware: 8×A100-80GB; SFT about 12 h for 2 epochs at lr 1e-5; GRPO about 20 h for 15 epochs at lr 5e-6, G=6, KL 0.05.
  - Inference: output tokens per step rise by about 45%, and trajectory time by about 26% at 3B.
  - Source: [VeriGUI Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05477)
- **Stated limitation.** Training assumes a failed action leaves the screen unchanged ("failure idempotency"), so wrong navigations and partial transitions are not modelled. — [VeriGUI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05477)

**BacktrackAgent** (arXiv 2505.20660, 2025-05-27; Qwen2-VL-7B)
- **Data.** The generator re-generates actions at every step of the *training* set. Wrong actions plus their resulting page train a judger (wrong vs right). The reflector is trained on 100% of the ineffective actions and 20% of the effective ones. On Mobile3M this yields 27,463 negatives against 259,725 positives. No external teacher is used; the negatives come from the model's own regenerated actions. — [BacktrackAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20660)
- **Results on Mobile3M** (task success rate): Qwen2-VL SFT 44.81, ReachAgent (SFT + DPO) 46.52, BacktrackAgent 54.11. Backtracking alone adds +5.65. — [BacktrackAgent Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20660)
- **Key failure mode.** With *simulated* execution pages (needed for chain datasets like Auto-UI that lack the real page after a wrong action), backtracking added only +0.7 task success rate and "caused a decrease in task-level and step-level accuracy". The real pages come from Mobile3M's graph-structured data. — [BacktrackAgent §5.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20660)
- **Cost.**
  - Training on 8×A100: 25 h for 2 epochs on Mobile3M, 97 h on Auto-UI.
  - Inference: the full agent runs at about 0.45-0.52× the generator's speed.
  - Source: [BacktrackAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20660)

**DeLoop: the agent's own loops as DPO pairs** (arXiv 2610.19376, 2026-10-14; language/tool agents)
- **Pair mining.** A looping action (an exact repeat with no new information) is the rejected response. The first later action in the *same rollout* that differs and does not loop is the chosen one, with the same pre-loop history as the prompt. There is "no task reward, gold label, or LLM judge". If the agent never recovers within the rollout, no pair is made. — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19376)
- **Training.** LoRA DPO, β 0.05, one epoch on 8,000 self-mined CodeGym pairs (Qwen3-8B). — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19376)
- **Results** (CodeGym, history compressed at 4,096 tokens):

  | Model and method | Loop rate | Success | Steps per task |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Qwen3-8B baseline | 57.3% | 25.1 | 15.0 |
  | Qwen3-8B DeLoop | 6.1% | 25.9 | 4.67 |
  | Llama-3.1-8B baseline | 43.7% | 17.8 | 17.51 |
  | Llama-3.1-8B DeLoop | 2.0% | 19.4 | 5.72 |
  | Qwen3-32B **(>8B)**, full history, base | 25.0% | 46.3 | — |
  | Qwen3-32B **(>8B)**, full history, DeLoop | 11.7% | 51.3 | — |

  Source: [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19376)
- **Baselines that did *not* fix loops** (Qwen3-8B): gold-action GRPO 52.7% loops; SFT on solved traces 42.7%; an exact-duplicate blocker at inference 46.7%; Reflexion when stuck 55.3%. — [DeLoop Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19376)
- **Random-pair control.** Random later-action pairs from the same rollouts left loops at 54.8% after one epoch and 69.5% after nine (worse than the 66.1% baseline). "What breaks the loop is the loop label, not preference training on the agent's own rollouts." — [DeLoop Table 27](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19376)

**GUI-Reflection** (arXiv 2506.08012, 2025-06-09; InternVL2.5-8B)
- **Offline data from AndroidControl:**
  - An MLLM edits the goal so that a gold step becomes a mistake, followed by Back and a retry (17,557 samples).
  - Ineffective actions are inserted, followed by reflection (15,394 samples).
- **Results.** On its own environment: 14.58 → 23.61 with reflection data, and 34.72 with online reflection tuning. AW 34.5.
- **Teacher.** The goal edits and reflections need an MLLM.
- Source: [GUI-Reflection](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08012) (prior project notes)

**Other recovery and reflection data methods (all need a teacher or an environment)**
- **RoTS** (arXiv 2605.29447, 2026-05-28): a tree-based pipeline that finds error modes and synthesises recovery steps, producing 800K samples. RoTS-7B and RoTS-32B; OSWorld 47.4 at 32B **(>8B)**. Needs live environments. — [RoTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29447)
- **STeP** (arXiv 2505.20023, 2025-05-26): self-reflected trajectories with corrected error steps, plus *partial masking* so that wrong steps are not learned. The teacher is Qwen1.5-110B **(>8B teacher)** and the student LLaMA2-7B, on ALFWorld, WebShop and SciWorld. — [STeP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20023)
- **Mobile-Agent-E** (arXiv 2501.11733, 2025-01-20): a hierarchical multi-agent framework (Manager, Perceptor, Operator, Action Reflector, Notetaker) whose memory of "Tips" and "Shortcuts" persists across tasks. It reports +22% absolute on Mobile-Eval-E across three large backbones. This is inference-time self-evolution, not weight training. — [Mobile-Agent-E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11733)

**Hindsight relabelling (all use an auxiliary model)**
- **HSL** (arXiv 2607.04235, 2026-07-05): an auxiliary LLM relabels each trajectory with the goals it actually achieved, with irrelevant-action masking and reweighting. On ALFWorld it beats baselines using a quarter of the demonstrations. — [HSL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04235)
- **SOLO** (arXiv 2609.34321, 2026-09-28): a judge keeps successes, and a proposer-verifier pair relabels a failed prefix with the subtask it completed. Then top-K self-distillation on the agent's own predictions. +3 to +6 points with UI-TARS-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B on WebArena, VisualWebArena and MobileWorld task streams. — [SOLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34321)
- **AgentBrew** (arXiv 2609.05837, 2026-09-05): infers the task in hindsight and uses PMI per-action credit. Qwen3-32B **(>8B)** +8.7 accuracy, beating rejection sampling (+5.9). — [AgentBrew](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05837)

### Inferences
- VeriGUI's "timing error" type (`wait` where interaction is needed, 15%) and its "null click" type match our 21% `wait`-under-a-non-wait-intent failure. All five failure types can be generated **by rules, without GPT-4o**:
  - Coordinate offset: jitter the gold point outside the element box.
  - Action-type error: swap the type.
  - Wrong target: tap a different element from the AC or AMEX accessibility tree.
  - Timing error: emit `wait`.
  - Null click: tap blank space.

  Keep the screen unchanged, and use the gold action as the label with a short templated intent such as "previous tap had no effect; …". This removes the teacher but also VeriGUI's GPT-4o-written diagnosis. The paper does not isolate how much of its gain came from the diagnosis text.
- Use about 30% negatives, not 50%. VeriGUI's 50:50 run lost 2.5 points of step SR for no recovery-success gain, and 10% gave almost no recovery gain.
- BacktrackAgent's simulated-page result warns that negatives with a *fabricated* next screen can hurt. AC, GUI-Odyssey and AMEX are chain datasets, so only the "unchanged screen" assumption (no-op failures) is safe without an emulator. Do not invent "wrong page" screens.
- DeLoop's pair rule maps naturally onto our emulator rollouts. The rejected action is a repeated identical intent+action with no screen change. The chosen action is the first later different action. It needs rollouts on *clean, non-AndroidWorld* tasks, and loops the agent never breaks yield no pairs. Even at 8B it cut loops by 9× without raising success, so expect fewer wasted steps rather than more AW wins.
- Hindsight relabelling without a teacher could reuse AndroidControl's step-level low-level instructions: relabel a prefix with the concatenated low-level instructions it completed. That would be teacher-free, but untested. Every published relabelling method uses an auxiliary LLM.

### Gaps
- No paper reports offline recovery data at ≤2B. VeriGUI-3B is the smallest.
- VeriGUI does not report AW for its Standard-SFT vs Robust-SFT arms at 3B, so the online effect of the negative data alone is unknown.
- No paper tests rule-generated diagnoses or intents against GPT-4o-generated ones.
- DeLoop has no GUI or vision results, and no result below 8B.

## Q4. Offline RL and preference methods on static data: gains over SFT at small scale, and when they hurt

### Takeaway
Two recent methods train on static data with no environment and no external teacher.
- **Agentic-DPO** samples one-step "student mistakes" at expert states and runs DPO against the expert action, with an SFT anchor. At Qwen3.5-2B it raised Mind2Web average step success from 35.4 (SFT) to 52.3, beating ETO (trajectory DPO, 48.5) and, on τ-bench, GRPO. It costs about 1.6× SFT per step.
- **GHD** uses the same model shown the next screenshot as a self-teacher. At 7-8B it added +6 AW and +11 to +15 AndroidLab over SFT, where plain GRPO added +0.6 / +2.3 AW and *lowered* AndroidLab at 8B. GHD still needs a GRPO rollout stack.

Where offline methods hurt:
- GRPO on gold history: flat AW in UI-S1, lower AndroidLab in GHD, and lower AC step accuracy in our own run.
- KL-free GRPO (reward hacking, per GUI-Libra).
- DPO without an SFT warm-up or anchor (collapse to 0% at 2B and 4B).
- Too many preference rounds at 2B (overfitting).
- Too many negatives in SFT.

### Cited Findings
**Agentic-DPO: offline step-level DPO against the student's own one-step mistakes** (arXiv 2607.10601, 2026-07-12; code public)
- **Method.**
  - Start with an SFT warm-up.
  - At each expert state s_t (gold history), sample K=4 one-step actions from the *current student*. Drop unparseable candidates and those equivalent to the expert action. Keep the highest-probability wrong action as the rejected response; the expert action is chosen.
  - Loss: DPO + λ·SFT (λ 0.5, β 0.008), with R=5 "negative-refresh" rounds that resample negatives from the updated student.
  - No environment execution, reward model or external model during training.
  - Hardware: 4×A6000.
  - Source: [Agentic-DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601)
- **Policy-Preserving Augmentation (PPA)** renders the same trajectory under several action schemas. This includes "recovery-context PPA", which changes the surrounding context while keeping the policy fixed. — [Agentic-DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601)
- **Results** (mean of 3 seeds):

  | Backbone | Method | Mind2Web avg step success | τ-bench retail SR |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Qwen3.5-2B | SFT | 35.4 | 18.0 |
  | Qwen3.5-2B | PPA + SFT | 42.1 | 22.1 |
  | Qwen3.5-2B | ETO (trajectory DPO) | 48.5 | 28.5 |
  | Qwen3.5-2B | GRPO (online) | — | 32.1 |
  | Qwen3.5-2B | **Agentic-DPO** | **52.3** | **35.2** |
  | Qwen3.5-4B | SFT → Agentic-DPO | 38.6 → 54.5 | 20.4 → 38.5 |
  | Qwen3.5-9B **(>8B)** | SFT → Agentic-DPO | 45.6 → 64.4 | 21.7 → 41.4 (GRPO 40.0) |

  GRPO was not run on Mind2Web because "static traces lack the step-level reward GRPO needs". The extract does not say whether Mind2Web inputs were screenshots or HTML. — [Agentic-DPO Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601)
- **Failure modes** (StableToolBench ablation):
  - Without the SFT warm-up, accuracy is 0.0% on both Qwen3.5-2B and Gemma3-4B: samples drift off the action schema.
  - Without the SFT anchor during DPO, accuracy is 14-18 points below the full recipe.
  - The 2B model "begins to overfit after its peak" number of refresh rounds.
  - Most of the gain comes from finding *one* plausible mistake per state: K=2-4 suffices.
  - Agentic-DPO "cannot discover new states induced by the student's own long-horizon behavior".
  - Source: [Agentic-DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601)
- **Cost per step** (relative to SFT): Agentic-DPO 1.6×, GRPO 13.6× (Qwen3.5-9B, τ-bench). — [Agentic-DPO §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601)

**GHD: gated hindsight self-distillation** (arXiv 2608.06065, 2026-08-06; code "will be made available")
- **Method.**
  - A teacher that shares the student's weights also sees the next gold screenshot o_{t+1}. It re-scores the student's own sampled responses.
  - Token-level distillation (JSD on the top-100 logits, λ=0.1, added to the GRPO loss) applies only when the student's step reward falls below threshold *and* the teacher's top-1 tokens recover the demonstrated action (coordinates within 20 on a 1000-point grid).
  - At most 3 resampling attempts per batch.
  - Source: [GHD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06065)
- **Data and compute.**
  - Data: a hard subset of the OpenMobile training set (27,360 examples, filtered to 6,968 for 7B and 5,982 for 8B) of steps the SFT model fails.
  - Compute: 4×A100 or RTX PRO 6000; G=8; 200 optimisation steps; lr 1e-6; no KL; screenshots 420×896.
  - Source: [GHD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06065)
- **Results** (Pass@1, 3 runs):

  | Model | SFT | + GRPO | + GHD |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Qwen2.5-VL-7B, AW | 46.55 | 47.13 | 52.73 |
  | Qwen2.5-VL-7B, AndroidLab | 29.71 | 31.93 | 43.10 |
  | Qwen3-VL-8B, AW | 59.05 | 61.35 | 66.47 |
  | Qwen3-VL-8B, AndroidLab | 39.13 | **37.43** | 54.11 |

  Source: [GHD Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06065)
- **Ablation** (7B AW, gain over GRPO): gate with an unprivileged teacher +0.71; plus dynamic sampling +2.43; plus the next screenshot (full GHD) +5.60. — [GHD Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06065)

**Trajectory-level and tree-level preference methods (need rollouts)**
- **ETO** (arXiv 2403.02502, 2024-03-04): explores in the environment, pairs failed with successful trajectories, and trains with DPO, over iterative rounds. — [ETO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02502)
- Within Agentic-DPO's comparison, ETO had high variance at 9B on τ-bench (20.9 ± 11.8). — [Agentic-DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601)
- **MobileIPL** (arXiv 2505.12299, 2025-05-18): builds a tree of reasoning steps by iterative sampling, scores leaves with rule-based rewards, and derives thinking-level DPO pairs. Instruction evolution uses GPT-4o. Effect sizes were not extracted. — [MobileIPL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12299)

**When offline RL hurts (new evidence plus a short recap)**
- In DeLoop, gold-action GRPO left the loop rate at 52.7% (baseline 57.3%) with success 25.0 (baseline 25.1). A gold-match reward does not target repetition. — [DeLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19376)
- Agentic-DPO's explanation of weak GRPO on static agent data: "on many states the group of G=4 rollouts shares the same correctness label and yields zero advantage signal". — [Agentic-DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601)
- Recap of evidence already in the project notes:
  - UI-S1 offline GRPO: AC-High 52.7 → 59.0 but AW 14.9 → 15.7. — [UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)
  - GUI-Libra-3B: KL=0 gives rising reward but falling AC and AW (KL 0 → 21.7 AW vs KL 1e-3 → 25.2). — [GUI-Libra, arXiv 2602.22190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22190)
  - AgentCPM-GUI-8B RFT left AC-High flat (69.5 → 69.2). — [AgentCPM-GUI, arXiv 2506.01391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01391)
  - Ferret-UI Lite-3B: step-wise RLVR gave AW +3 (25 → 28). — [Ferret-UI Lite, arXiv 2509.26539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26539)
- **PACT** (arXiv 2611.22591, 2026-11-17): masks negative policy-gradient on reasoning spans whose plan matches the reference, and repairs only the action. AW rose from 60.34 to 68.97 over an outcome-only baseline. The model size was not extracted, but the baseline level suggests ≥7B. — [PACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22591)

### Inferences
- Our GRPO result (0.728 → 0.667 AC step accuracy while answer accuracy rose) matches several documented mechanisms:
  - Reward weighting that favours the answer component.
  - Zero-variance groups on most AC steps (the 2B SFT model already gets about 0.73 right).
  - Possibly no or low KL.

  Agentic-DPO and GHD avoid the zero-variance problem: DPO always has a pair, and GHD distils only on failures.
- Agentic-DPO fits our stack and budget best. It needs:
  - Only our existing static data.
  - K=4 one-step samples per state from the current model. Generation can run on the Mac or the A100.
  - A DPO trainer with an SFT anchor.

  Two adaptations seem natural. First, sample negatives on **self-history states** (UI-S1 rollout, Q1), not gold-history states, so that copied intents and spurious `wait`s show up as negatives. Second, keep the SFT anchor (λ about 0.5) and stop after 1-2 refresh rounds at 2B. Neither change has been tested.
- GHD's self-teacher is teacher-free and uses the next screenshot, which every AC, GUI-Odyssey and AMEX trajectory has. It needs GRPO rollouts and a second forward pass, plus code that is not yet released. Within a day on one A100 at 2B it is feasible only as a small run of about 200 steps, mirroring GHD's own budget at 7B on 4 GPUs. This is an extrapolation.

### Gaps
- Agentic-DPO has not been tested on a mobile benchmark or on AW. Whether its Mind2Web setting used screenshots is unconfirmed.
- No offline preference method reports AW or AndroidLab at ≤4B.
- GHD has no result below 7B, and its code is unreleased.
- PACT's model size and training data were not extracted.

## Q5. Which methods use only the agent's own rollouts on the training set, and which need a verifier or teacher? Practical shortlist for ~1 day on 1×A100 plus a Mac with two emulators

### Takeaway
Seven methods need **no external teacher or judge and no online environment**:
1. Self-history SFT (UI-S1's rollout with an SFT loss)
2. UI-S1 semi-online RL
3. SOLAR-RL
4. Agentic-DPO
5. GHD (self-teacher)
6. Rule-generated VeriGUI-style no-op negatives
7. HiconAgent history-length sampling

BacktrackAgent's self-generated negatives are teacher-free but need real next pages, which our chain datasets lack. DeLoop is teacher-free but needs environment rollouts. Everything else in this catalogue needs a large teacher or judge (GPT-4o, Gemini, Kimi, an MLLM editor) or a live environment.

### Inferences
**Ranking for our constraints** (1 day, 1×A100, the Mac and 2 emulators, no AW tasks, no paid teacher). Each item is a judgement from the evidence above, not a reported result.

1. **Self-history SFT ("UI-S1 rollout, SFT loss").**
   - How: roll v-current through about 1-3K AC, GUI-Odyssey and AMEX *train* episodes with its own intent history (llama.cpp on the Mac or vLLM on the A100). Save (own history, gold screen, gold label) at mismatches, patch with ε=1, and SFT on the mix.
   - Cost: generation plus one normal SFT run. Fits in a day. Teacher-free and contamination-safe.
   - Risk: the gain is untested (UI-S1 used RL).
2. **Add rule-generated VeriGUI-style no-op negatives at about 30%.** These target `wait` and null-tap repeats directly. Use templated intents rather than GPT-4o diagnoses. Risks: about 1 point of step SR, and unknown effectiveness without the diagnosis text.
3. **Agentic-DPO on self-history states.**
   - How: run K=4 samples per state; the negatives are the model's own copied intents or wrong `wait`s, and the chosen action is gold. Keep the SFT anchor; 1-2 refresh rounds.
   - Cost: about 1.6× SFT per step. Feasible in a day at 2B if the DPO trainer handles our image format.
   - Risks: collapse without the anchor, and overfitting at 2B after the peak round.
4. **DeLoop pairs from the emulators** on clean non-AW tasks. Only worth doing if rollouts show the agent sometimes breaks its own loops; otherwise few pairs come out. Expect fewer wasted steps rather than more successes.
5. **GHD or full UI-S1 RL.** Teacher-free and the best-evidenced static methods for AW at 7-8B, but both need a GRPO/verl stack ported to LFM3-VL-2B. That is unlikely to be done and debugged within one day. Keep these as the next step after 1-3 show movement on the SOP proxy.

**Things to avoid, given the evidence:**
- Plain GRPO on gold history: flat AW, lower AndroidLab at 8B, our own AC drop.
- KL=0.
- DPO without an SFT anchor.
- Negatives with fabricated "wrong page" screenshots on chain datasets.
- More than about 30% negatives.
- Inference-only outcome notes that were never in training.

### Gaps
- No method in the catalogue reports AW, or any online benchmark, below 3B. The 2B evidence (Agentic-DPO) is from Mind2Web and tool-use benchmarks, not mobile.
- Nobody has reproduced any of these methods independently. All gains are author-reported, and the AC-High protocols of different papers disagree (UI-S1-7B: 68.2 self-reported vs 44.5 under VeriGUI's protocol).
- No source measures intent-copying (repeating the previous thought verbatim) as a failure metric for GUI agents. The closest are VeriGUI's Loop Rate and DeLoop's exact-action repeats.
