# Shift training from imitation to self-correction

LFM3-VL-2B's AndroidWorld ceiling comes from what it is trained on and what it is shown at each step. The backbone and the hyperparameters are not what holds it back. The same LoRA recipe on Qwen3.5-2B scores **16/114 (provisional)** against LFM3's **15/116**, and the two models solve mostly the same short settings-toggle and delete tasks. AndroidControl low-level accuracy (0.948) is near its ceiling while AndroidWorld (AW) sits at 12.9%. Each of the four failure modes is a gap that offline, gold-history imitation cannot fill. AndroidControl has no `answer` labels, and our agent emitted **zero answers in 116 episodes**, which gives up most of the roughly quarter of AW that is information retrieval. Gold histories never contain the agent's own mistakes. A text-only history cannot tell the model whether its last action did anything. The literature agrees: offline step accuracy explains under half the variance in AW success (R² 0.47), while training on the agent's own history or own rollouts is the step most consistently tied to AW gains at 2-7B. Online RL at published scale is out of reach with roughly 4-5 GPU-hours and two emulators, so the plan runs in a fixed order. First, build cheap offline proxies and calibrate them against the models already run on AW. Then spend the first post-v2 GPU run on failure-targeted data: navigate-then-answer episodes, injected no-op recovery, masked loop labels, and outcome notes trained into the history. The second run adds one previous screenshot and a higher resolution. The third is one on-policy round of DAgger-style corrections and verified successes, collected on free Mac and emulator time. GRPO waits until there is budget for it. Extrapolating from 3-8B Qwen-based results (not a measured 2B number), the three runs should plausibly move the agent from 15 to about **20-30/116**, at or above GUI-Owl-1.5-2B's 23/116 in our harness.

## Same recipe, same ceiling: the backbone and hyperparameters are not binding

Our own runs point to the recipe, not the model, as the bottleneck. Qwen3.5-2B trained with the identical recipe reaches AndroidControl (AC) 0.734/0.949 against LFM3's 0.715/0.948 ([STATUS](https://github.com/alpharomercoma/lfm3-android-runs/blob/archive/pre-restructure-2026-11-20/STATUS.md)). On AW, the in-progress Qwen run has **16 successes in 114 episodes**, and **10 of those 16 tasks are also among LFM3's 15 successes**. Both sets are dominated by Wi-Fi/Bluetooth/brightness toggles, single-item deletes and open-app tasks ([Qwen run](../../results/androidworld/qwen35_2b_ft/run_20261117T111259609427), [LFM3 failure modes](../../results/androidworld/lfm3vl2b_ft.failure_modes.json)). LFM3 solves **14 of 61 easy tasks, 0 of 36 medium and 1 of 19 hard** ([LFM3 summary](../../results/androidworld/lfm3vl2b_ft.summary.json)). Two different 2B backbones under one recipe hitting the same narrow ceiling means the recipe sets the ceiling.

Offline accuracy does not predict that ceiling. Our AC-High of 0.715 already beats MAI-UI-2B (67.3) and Ferret-UI Lite-3B (68.9), which score 49.1 and 28.0 on AW in their own harnesses ([MAI-UI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22047), [Ferret-UI Lite](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26539)). UI-S1 measured this across models: AC-High predicts AW with **R² = 0.470**, GUI-Odyssey with 0.398. AgentCPM-GUI-8B is the cautionary case, with AC-High 69.2 but **16.4 on AW** ([UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)). Adding more AC-style data follows the same pattern. The AndroidControl authors extrapolate that out-of-domain high-level step accuracy reaches 95% only at around **60M episodes**, against 1M in-domain ([AndroidControl](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03679)).

The hyperparameters already follow the best general evidence. The adapter is **LoRA r64/α128 at lr 2e-4 on every language-model linear layer, including the MLP and conv projections** ([v2 job](../../scripts/training/jobs/lfm3_v2mix_g4.json)). "LoRA Without Regret" found that attention-only LoRA "significantly underperforms", that the best LoRA learning rate is about 10x the full fine-tuning rate, and that LoRA is less tolerant of large batches ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)), and our setup satisfies all three points. Rank is not the lever either: in AndroidControl's PaLM-2S study, raising LoRA rank *widened* the in-domain vs out-of-domain gap, from −6.8 at r4 to −12.3 at r64 ([AndroidControl](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03679)). Two questions remain open, neither tested on GUI agents at 2B. One is whether the frozen vision tower costs anything. The other is action-token loss weighting, which GUI-Libra credits with 5.2→13.0 on AW at 3B; that baseline used long CoT that hurt grounding, so the gain should be smaller for our short-intent format ([GUI-Libra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22190)). One budget effect matters when reading v2: the fixed 1,250 steps draw 40K samples from a 79K-row mix, so v2 sees about 28K AC rows against v1's 40K. A small AC-test drop would therefore be expected and would not mean the new sources hurt.

The failure modes map onto missing supervision and hidden outcomes, not onto capacity. LFM3's 101 failures split into **54 step-budget exhaustions** (28 with three or more consecutive `wait`s, 16 with app names the device does not have, such as "Music" or "File Manager") and **45 `complete` claims on failed tasks**. **No episode contained an `answer` action** ([LFM3 failure modes](../../results/androidworld/lfm3vl2b_ft.failure_modes.json)). AW has **27 information-retrieval tasks out of 116** ([OPSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27307v1)), and GUI-Owl's extra wins over us are mostly question-answer tasks ([STATUS](https://github.com/alpharomercoma/lfm3-android-runs/blob/archive/pre-restructure-2026-11-20/STATUS.md)).

GUI-Owl's failure profile in the same harness is the mirror image. It never ended an episode itself, emitted answers in 11 episodes, and ran out its budget in 92 ([GUI-Owl failure modes](../../results/androidworld/guiowl15_2b_matched.failure_modes.json)). This points to an asymmetry in AW's scoring: success is checked on the final device state, so running out the budget after reaching the goal costs nothing, while a false `complete` ends the episode and locks in the failure. The labels describe outcomes, not causes, so it is unknown how many of the 45 false completions stopped short of a goal that more steps would have reached.

One harness experiment already supports fixing this in training rather than at inference. The opt-in runtime-feedback run resolves app names lexically and appends "[no visible change]" and failure notes to the history, notes the model never saw during training. Provisionally it has **7 successes in 103 episodes against v1's 12 on the same tasks**, and **only 1 of the 16 unmapped-app episodes became a success** ([runtime-feedback run](../../results/androidworld/lfm3vl2b_ft_runtimefb/run_20261117T112723045785)). The run is incomplete and has 7 infrastructure exceptions against v1's 2, so it is not conclusive. It does suggest two things: test-time annotations the model was not trained on do not help, and the app-name problem is mostly a symptom of policies that would fail later anyway.

| Candidate bottleneck | Verdict | Deciding evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Dataset (what is supervised) | **Primary** | Zero `answer` actions. No recovery or error states in gold data. AC out-of-domain scaling is extremely slow. |
| Training method (gold-history imitation) | **Primary** | Our offline step accuracy is above that of agents scoring 28-49 on AW. The R² of 0.47 shows offline accuracy does not predict AW. On-policy methods dominate the AW gains below. |
| Input design (history, outcomes, resolution) | **Secondary but cheap** | The text history cannot show whether an action worked. GUI-Owl-1.5-2B loses ~48 points between its own harness and ours. |
| Backbone | Not binding now | Qwen3.5-2B under the same recipe gives the same AW score and largely the same solved tasks. |
| Hyperparameters | Not binding | The LoRA setup matches the best general guidance. No AW-relevant hyperparameter evidence exists at 2B. |

## The literature prices each lever; on-policy data and visible outcomes lead

The levers that moved AW at 2-4B change *what the model trains on*, either its own rollouts or failure-specific data, rather than *how* it optimizes on the same offline data. Plain offline GRPO on gold-history steps adds little. In UI-S1's controlled 7B comparison it raised AC-High by 6.3 points but **AW by only 0.8** ([UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)). GHD measured +0.6 and +2.3 over SFT at 7B and 8B, with AndroidLab *falling* at 8B ([GHD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06065)), and Ferret-UI Lite got +3 at 3B ([Ferret-UI Lite](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26539)).

Training on the agent's own history is what moves the number. UI-S1's semi-online RL, which rolls the policy through expert trajectories with its own outputs in the history, took Qwen2.5-VL-7B from **21.7 (SFT) to 34.0** ([UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)).

OpenMobile's 7B ablation compares four ways of collecting data. Pure expert distillation scored 44.8 and filtered self-evolution 33.8. An **error-intervention switch scored 48.3**: the learner drives, an expert takes over when it deviates, and only expert steps are trained, *with the learner's errors left in the history* ([OpenMobile](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15093)). DigiRL's 1.3B agent on AitW went from 17.7% (SFT) to **57.8% with online filtered behaviour cloning** on about 1,000 rollouts ([DigiRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11896)). These SFT-loss methods matter to us because they reuse our existing LoRA SFT stack and spend emulator time, which we have, instead of GPU time, which we do not.

Full online RL is the most powerful lever at 2B and the least affordable. In-environment RL added **+4.0 to MAI-UI-2B** and **+14.5 to UI-Venus-1.5-2B** (before a merge that cost 4.2) ([MAI-UI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22047), [UI-Venus-1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09082)). Those runs used 32-512 concurrent environments. MAI-UI reports that fewer environments "exhibit early saturation" and that vanilla GRPO gave only +1.8 at 8B, against +6.0 with curriculum, replay and repetition penalties ([MAI-UI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22047)). The smallest documented win, MobileGym's 10 GRPO steps over about 960 trajectories, would take us roughly 11-20 emulator-hours per run. It was also measured on browser-simulated apps, not AW ([MobileGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26114)).

Input design matters most where our failures sit: noticing that an action did nothing. SecAgent-3B's offline ablation is the clearest small-model evidence. Adding one previous screenshot raised task accuracy from **36.2 to 72.8** for 45% more input tokens, with little further gain at five, and removing the natural-language "semantic context" summary cut the one-screenshot result to 56.4 ([SecAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08533)). OpenPhone-3B fell from 15.2 to 4.3 on AndroidLab without history ([OpenPhone](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22009)). Resolution is a smaller, secondary factor. MAI-UI found 720p matches 1080p while 540p "substantially degrades" performance ([MAI-UI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22047)), and our 774K-pixel budget (about 590×1310 on a 1080×2400 screen, ~720 image tokens) sits at the low end of that range. Prompt order is a free rider: Phi-Ground measured +1.7 to +2.4 on mobile grounding from putting the instruction before the image ([Phi-Ground](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23779)), and our prompt places the image first ([agent_format.py](../../train/agent_format.py)).

Short reasoning should stay, and action-only rows are a risk. Ferret-UI Lite-3B went from 13.7 on AW with no CoT to 15.8 with short and 19.6 with long CoT ([Ferret-UI Lite](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26539)), and GUI-Libra-3B fell from 25.2 to 12.2 without CoT ([GUI-Libra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22190)). At 4B, MIRAGE's **action-only SFT (31.0) scored below the untuned base (42.9)** ([MIRAGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04627)). That is a warning for v2, whose 7,469 AMEX rows have blank intents rendered as empty `<think></think>` ([v2 card](../../results/data_v2/README.md)).

## Four GPU-hours buy three runs, so each must stack proven fixes

The budget decides the method. At about 10 compute units per G4-hour, the remaining ~25 + ~10 Colab units come to **about 3.5 G4-hours**. Modal's ~$13 covers roughly one more v1-sized run, since v1 LFM3 and the 450M run together used about $13 on Modal (project memory). Qwen3.5-0.8B trains at 1.8 s/step on the G4 ([STATUS](https://github.com/alpharomercoma/lfm3-android-runs/blob/archive/pre-restructure-2026-11-20/STATUS.md)), so a 1,250-step 2B run plus evals should take about an hour, or about 1.5 hours with a second image (estimate). That allows three training runs and a small evaluation-and-generation session, not an RL campaign.

Emulator time is the resource we have in surplus. At ~85 episodes/hour, a full AW pass takes **about 1.4 hours on both emulators**, and one night yields **about 1,000 rollouts**, the scale at which DigiRL first showed large gains ([DigiRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11896)). Colab and Modal cannot host the emulators: Colab has no confirmed `/dev/kvm`, and Modal's nested KVM is off by default ([Modal docs](https://frontend.modal.com/docs/guide/vm-sandboxes)). The design therefore moves work onto the Mac.

**Step 2, the first training run,** carries the cheap, well-evidenced data fixes. It needs no new infrastructure and keeps the current single-image format. The largest piece is navigate-then-answer episodes: rewrite held AC and GUI-Odyssey goals into questions whose answer is visible on the final screenshot, as Ferret-UI Lite and OpenMobile do, and end each episode with `answer(...)` followed by `status complete`. UI-Venus rebuilt its information-retrieval traces the same way to insert explicit answers ([UI-Venus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10833)). v2's ScreenQA rows teach the `answer` format only on single screens, so they do not cover this pattern. No-op recovery needs no emulator either. GUI-Reflection built its "ineffective action" samples from AndroidControl: add an action that cannot change the screen (a scroll at a list boundary, a tap on blank space) to the history, keep the same screenshot, and keep the gold action as the label ([GUI-Reflection](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08012)). Loop masking removes the labels on consecutive identical actions and on `wait` runs while keeping those steps in the history, because Xiaomi-GUI-0 found that using repeated steps as labels "teaches the model … to keep repeating when stuck" ([Xiaomi-GUI-0](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31410v2)). Finally, compute the screen difference between consecutive gold screenshots and write into the training history the same "[no visible change]" note the runtime harness already produces, so the note stops being a test-time surprise. GUI-Libra-style upweighting of action tokens is an optional, cheap addition; expect less than its 3B gain, because our thoughts are already short.

**Step 3, the second training run,** tests the largest input-design lever. It costs inference latency, which the project plans to recover later through ViT embedding caching, a technique that has been shown to keep accuracy ([Where and How to Prune](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26041)). Because it changes the image layout anyway, it is also the right run in which to move the instruction ahead of the image.

**Step 4, the on-policy round,** goes after the offline/online mismatch directly, using two data streams.

The first stream runs on the Mac with llama.cpp and no emulator. Roll the best model through ~1,500 AC and GUI-Odyssey *training* episodes using its own intent history. At the first mismatch with the gold action, record a training sample made of the model's own history plus the gold label. Then patch once with the gold action and continue, as in UI-S1's thought-free patch with ε=1. This is UI-S1's rollout scheme with an SFT loss instead of its RL objective, so the size of the gain is untested.

The second stream uses the emulators. Collect four rollouts per task at temperature 0.7-1.0 on a clean, rule-checked pool (see the contamination section) and keep tasks with 0 < pass@4 < 1 as the curriculum. Keep verified successes, shortest first. Mine fork points where a success and a failure reach the same screen, using UI-Voyager's SSIM matching, and train the successful action in the failed context. UI-Voyager reports **73.2→81.0** from this step, beating GRPO and PPO, which plateaued at 76, although on AW-template tasks we would forbid ([UI-Voyager](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24533)).

At a 13% starting success rate, filtered self-rollouts alone will yield little. OpenMobile's self-evolution arm was its weakest. The gold-patched static stream is therefore the main source and the emulator stream the supplement.

If budget returns, the evidence favours three RL variants over plain GRPO. The first is semi-online GRPO with γ=0.5, a format/type/parameter reward split of 0.1/0.4/0.5 with a dense click term, and zero-variance filtering ([UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)). The second is GUI-Libra's offline GRPO with KL 1e-3, its best setting at 3B (KL=0 gave 21.7 against 25.2), and success-adaptive negative-gradient scaling ([GUI-Libra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22190)). The third is GHD's next-screenshot hindsight teacher, which added +6.2 and +7.4 over SFT at 7B and 8B ([GHD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06065)).

A deterministic loop breaker at inference is cheap insurance, but evaluate it as a harness change reported apart from model gains. VLAA-GUI lost 7.8 points on WindowsAgentArena without one, though that is desktop evidence with frontier backbones ([VLAA-GUI](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21375v3)).

## Screen candidates offline before spending emulator hours

A single 116-task AW pass cannot resolve the effects this plan targets. With an exact paired McNemar test, a net gain of 8 tasks reaches p<0.05 only with at most two regressions: 9 wins against 1 loss gives p≈0.02, but 12 against 4 gives p≈0.08. Our own GUI-Owl comparison, 16 against 8, came out at p=0.15 ([STATUS](https://github.com/alpharomercoma/lfm3-android-runs/blob/archive/pre-restructure-2026-11-20/STATUS.md)). AW therefore has to be a milestone check on pre-screened candidates, not the screening tool.

The primary proxy is UI-S1's semi-online performance (SOP) metric. It runs AC-test episodes with the *model's own* intent-and-action history, advances to the gold next screenshot only while actions match, stops at the first mismatch, and reports the fraction of steps completed and the fraction of tasks completed. UI-S1 found it tracks AW with **R² = 0.934**, against 0.470 for AC-High ([UI-S1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11543)). It runs on a fixed subsample of a few hundred episodes on the Mac through llama.cpp, or in minutes inside any GPU session. Trust it only after it ranks our existing models in the same coarse order AW does: base 1, 450M-FT 2-3, then v1 15 and Qwen-FT 16, which are tied within noise.

Four probes measure the targeted failures directly. The answer probe tracks emission rate and exact-match accuracy on held-out navigate-then-answer episodes and ScreenQA dev. The premature-terminal probe counts how often the model emits `status complete` on non-terminal SOP steps (v1's AC terminal accuracy is 0.792). The recovery probe measures how often the model repeats an injected ineffective action on AC-test steps. The app probe checks what share of `open_app` names resolve to the device's installed labels. AC test step accuracy remains a regression guard only: a drop of more than ~2 points flags damage, and a rise means nothing for AW.

Before AW, run an online smoke test on AW's bundled MiniWoB++ suite (`--suite_family=miniwob`). It shares the emulator stack and rule-based rewards but is a different task family ([android_world](https://github.com/google-research/android_world)). Then run AW once per candidate and compare it pairwise to v1 on identical instances. Re-run infrastructure exceptions instead of counting them as failures; the runtime-feedback run's 7 exceptions show how much noise they add. For a final headline number, add 2-3 AW *evaluation* seeds to tighten the interval, since re-seeding for evaluation, unlike training, is legitimate.

## Contamination rules keep the AndroidWorld number clean

Many headline AW numbers come from training on the benchmark's own task templates, which this project forbids. MobileRL trained on 2,000 re-parameterized AW tasks ([MobileRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18119)) and UI-Voyager on over 7,000 ([UI-Voyager](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24533)). ADMIRE runs its RL inside the AW sandbox ([ADMIRE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11524)), and ZeroGUI does test-time training on test tasks ([ZeroGUI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23762)). The gap is large. In AndroidWorld-Generalization, GRPO gains shrank from **+26.1 on unseen instances to +15.7 on unseen templates and +8.3 on unseen apps**, and **38 of the 116 templates produce identical instances regardless of seed** ([AndroidWorld-Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07432)). Any template-derived training would therefore partly train on exact test items. The rules below keep our numbers comparable to a clean protocol and should be logged alongside every training set.

| Data or practice | Rule |
|---|---|
| AW task instances, any seed or parameters, and AW task classes' `generate_random_params` or init logic | Forbidden for training data, rollouts, curricula or rewards |
| Our own AW evaluation trajectories (`results/androidworld/**`), including failures | Forbidden as training data, including as DAgger corrections or negatives |
| AW success checkers as a reward or filter for training data | Forbidden. Write separate adb/SQLite checks for self-made tasks. |
| Test-time training on AW tasks | Forbidden |
| AW MiniWoB++ suite, AndroidLab apps with no AW counterpart (Bluecoins, Cantook, Maps.me, PiMusic, Zoom) | Allowed for rollouts and training; report as clean |
| Self-generated tasks on AW's own apps | Allowed only after instruction dedup against all 116 template strings (n-gram plus embedding similarity), with a nearest-neighbour-removal ablation as in OpenMobile ([OpenMobile](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15093)); report as "AW-app overlap" |
| QA rewrites of AC and GUI-Odyssey goals | Allowed; dedup against AW's information-retrieval templates. Keep v2's exclusion of AW-app ScreenQA screens. |
| AndroidLab and MobileWorld *test* tasks | Evaluation only |
| Teacher or judge models (e.g. GUI-Owl) | Allowed on clean tasks only. Note that their own RL task pools are undisclosed. Never run them on AW tasks to produce training data. |
| The installed-app label list in prompts | Allowed: it is device metadata, not task content |
| AW used for model selection | Limit to one pre-registered pass per training run and report every pass, including failures |

Two further rules apply whenever a judge stands in for rule checks. Judge on screenshots and never show the judge the agent's own success claim; ZeroGUI found that including it raised recall but lowered precision and final success. Require unanimous votes, because false positives hurt training more than false negatives ([ZeroGUI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23762)). In matched GRPO, rule-based rewards beat the best judge on the full AW suite, **36.8 vs 30.2** ([MobileJudgeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11434)). A false positive in a filtered-BC set becomes a wrong demonstration, so tune for precision even if many real successes are discarded.

## Conclusion

The project's own data changed the question. Before the Qwen3.5-2B parity result, a weak backbone was a reasonable suspect. Now two 2B models on one recipe share one narrow set of successes, and the explanation has moved from "the model cannot" to "the model was never shown". It was never shown an answer, a mistake, a no-op, or its own history, and in the single-image format it often cannot see whether its last action did anything. That makes the cheapest fixes also the most targeted: supervision for answering, recovering and stopping, plus a history that reports action outcomes. The method question then becomes *whose rollouts the model learns from* rather than SFT versus RL, and our two emulators and idle Mac can provide those rollouts for free.

The bigger risk is measurement, not method. A 116-task pass cannot separate +5 from noise, offline step accuracy barely predicts AW, and the only well-validated proxy (SOP) was validated on other, larger agents. Calibrating SOP against the four models already run on AW in our format, plus v2 once its pass lands, is therefore the most valuable zero-GPU step. Without it, the remaining GPU-hours would be spent on candidates nobody can tell apart.
